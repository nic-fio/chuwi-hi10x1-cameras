This series adds drivers for two GalaxyCore sensors found on the CHUWI
Hi10 X1, an Intel N100 (Alder Lake-N) tablet with an IPU6 ISP, plus the
two ipu-bridge entries that let the IPU6 build a CSI-2 graph for them:

  GC5035, 5 Mpixel, front,  2 CSI-2 lanes, ACPI HID GCTI5035
  GC8034, 8 Mpixel, rear,   4 CSI-2 lanes, ACPI HID GCTI8034

Both are also described for device tree, since neither part is x86 only.

Tested on the machine above: both sensors capture, and the images are in
focus, correctly coloured and free of artefacts. v4l2-compliance reports
45 of 46 tests passing on each subdev; the one failure is control event
subscription, which no recent mainline sensor driver implements either
(gc05a2, gc08a3, ov2740, ov08x40 and t4ka3 all fail the same test).

Patch 1/2: GC5035 binding and driver
Patch 3/4: GC8034 binding and driver
Patch 5:   ipu-bridge entries for both

An int3472 change that used to accompany this work is no longer part of
it: it goes to a different subsystem and, as it turns out, these drivers
do not need it. See "The clock" below.

Why the link frequencies are derived and not hardcoded
------------------------------------------------------

Both drivers compute V4L2_CID_LINK_FREQ at probe time as a fixed multiple
of the external clock, rather than carrying a constant. This is unusual
enough to deserve the reasoning.

The register tables program the PLL once and never touch it again, so the
link frequency is by construction a fixed multiple of whatever clock the
sensor is given. The multiplier belongs to the table; the input clock
belongs to the board. Hardcoding their product bakes one board into the
driver.

That is not a theoretical concern here, because both vendor sources got
the constant wrong for this platform:

  - the Intel Alder Lake-M patch this GC5035 driver takes its tables from
    declares 438 MHz, which is not a whole multiple of any plausible
    external clock;
  - the Rockchip BSP this GC8034 driver takes its tables from declares
    336 MHz, correct for its own 24 MHz clock but not for the 19.2 MHz an
    IPU6 platform provides.

Measuring the frame rate over 200 frames settles it. The GC5035 runs at
28.81 fps where 438 MHz predicts 29.88, which works out at 422.4 MHz,
exactly 19.2 MHz x 22. The GC8034 runs at 23.99 fps where the tabulated
30.00 predicts 30.00, a ratio of 0.7997 against the 0.8000 of 19.2/24,
so 268.8 MHz, exactly 19.2 MHz x 14. Both agree with the corresponding
pixel rates to within 0.04%, and for the GC5035 the ratio is unchanged
at three different VBLANK settings, which rules out a timing model error
and leaves only the constant.

With the multiplier in the driver, the same code is correct on a device
tree board clocking either part at 24 MHz, where the numbers become
528 MHz and 336 MHz respectively.

The clock
---------

The GC8034 tables come from a BSP that clocks the sensor at 24 MHz, while
the PCH IMGCLKOUT on this machine runs at 19.2 MHz. The tables work
unmodified at that rate: every timing simply scales by 0.8. An earlier
version of this work carried a patch teaching the int3472 clock to select
24 MHz, which the PCH can do; it is not needed and has been dropped.

Provenance
----------

Neither driver is new code throughout, and the register tables in
particular are reproduced from vendor sources.

The GC5035 tables come from the out-of-tree Alder Lake-M patch in
intel/ipu6-drivers, which derives from the ChromeOS series posted by
Tomasz Figa in September 2020, itself based on work from Bitland. The
copyright lines of Bitland, Google and Intel are carried in the file
header.

The GC8034 tables come from the Rockchip BSP (rockchip-linux/kernel,
develop-5.10), GPL-2.0. The Fuzhou Rockchip copyright line is carried in
the file header.

The conversion from the vendor {reg, val} form to cci_reg_sequence was
mechanical and is verifiable: a script re-reads the generated tables and
compares them register by register against the originals, so the
rewriting can be shown to be purely syntactic without a reviewer having
to check 400 lines by hand.

There is a precedent this list will remember: the GC5035 was posted
before, as v4 by Tomasz Figa on 2 September 2020, reviewed by Sakari
Ailus and Rob Herring, and silent after 8 September. What is different
now is that the hardware is available for testing and the driver has
been rewritten against current APIs: CCI accessors, the streams API,
subdev active state, and runtime PM.

What is not covered
-------------------

  - The PLL, D-PHY and analogue bias registers are undocumented vendor
    blobs. There is no public register guide for either sensor. They are
    reproduced unmodified and the drivers do not attempt to recompute
    them.
  - The GC8034 has a single mode, 3264x2448; the GC5035 a single mode,
    2592x1944. No binning or cropping modes were available to import.
  - GC8034 VTS is programmed through an offset register whose base is
    inferred from the BSP defaults, not measured. An error of a few lines
    there would shift the frame rate by about 0.1%, below what can be
    measured.
  - The GC8034 module carries a dw9714 VCM, which ipu-bridge instantiates.
    Focus control has not been exercised.
  - Both were tested on one machine, with one sample of each sensor.

Testing done
------------

  - both sensors capture at full resolution, images verified visually
  - analogue gain verified against the requested value: 16x requested and
    15.7x measured on the GC5035, 7.66x requested and 7.9x measured on
    the GC8034
  - GC5035 test pattern verified pixel-exact across a full frame, which
    also validates the CSI-2 packing
  - frame rate predicted by the driver matches the measured rate on both
  - v4l2-compliance, 45/46 as described above
  - ten bind/unbind cycles per driver, clean
  - checkpatch --strict, sparse, smatch and 69 coccinelle scripts: clean
  - make dt_binding_check and yamllint on both bindings: clean
